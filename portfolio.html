Advantech WISE Partner Program

Advantech worked with system integrators, software vendors, and resellers, but had no formal partner program in place. I contributed to the end-to-end development and implementation of a new partner program from the ground up, defining partner value, qualification criteria, and growth opportunities to establish a structured partner ecosystem.

Goal

Build a unified partner ecosystem that strengthens partner engagement, simplifies onboarding, and creates clear paths for growth.

My Role

  • Framework Research: Researched partner program models to define the program structure, benefits, and qualification criteria.
  • Brochure Development: Developed four partner brochures tailored to different partner groups with consistent messaging.
  • Website Planning: Led planning for 6 partner web pages, aligning engineering and stakeholders on information architecture, UX, and onboarding workflows.
  • Partner Onboarding: Managed Marketplace listings and onboarding for 10+ SI and ISV partners.

Project Flow

Benchmark and Frame the Problem
  • Compared industry partner programs across tier structures, incentive models, and partner–vendor collaboration.
  • Identified gaps between incentive design and actual partner engagement, translating these findings into guiding principles for the program framework.

Output: Industry benchmark and framework principles.

Segment Partners by What They Do
  • Defined three partner tracks: System Integrators, Software Vendors, and Channel Partners, while accommodating other collaborators such as AI accelerator partners.
  • Clarified each track’s target partner profile, business model, and role within the ecosystem, helping prospective partners quickly identify where they fit.
  • Validated the segmentation against existing partners to ensure each partner could be assigned to one clearly defined track.
Design Tiers and a Partner Growth Path
  • Established four partnership tiers — Authorized, Certified, Advanced, and Strategic — with clear milestones and qualification criteria for progression.
  • Structured tier benefits across three dimensions: Commercial, Marketing, and Technical Support, with increasing value at each level.
  • Connected partner enablement, joint go-to-market opportunities, and revenue growth into one operating model, helping partners progress from one-off projects to repeatable solutions.

Output: Partner tier framework and Partner Success Roadmap.

Turn the Framework into Partner-Facing Materials
  • Developed four English-language brochures: an overall program overview and dedicated guides for System Integrators, Software Vendors, and Channel Partners, using a consistent structure to communicate program value, tier benefits, qualification criteria, and application steps.
  • Independently presented the program to prospective partners in English, explaining its value proposition and partnership opportunities.

Output: Four-brochure set, versioned as 2026 V1.

Bring the Program into Practice
  • Led planning for six partner web pages, covering information architecture, application data requirements, and the backend onboarding workflow. Pages are currently in development.
  • Onboarded 10+ System Integrator and Software Vendor partners to the Marketplace, introducing the program and its benefits in English.
  • Built a standardized partner evaluation tool to assess applicants against consistent qualification criteria.

Output: Partner website plan, Marketplace partner onboarding, and partner evaluation tool.

Partner Evaluator: An AI Skill That Qualifies Partners

Evaluating each partner applicant previously required hours of manual research, with results varying by researcher. I standardized the evaluation process and built an AI skill, weda-partner-evaluator, to make partner qualification more consistent, efficient, and accessible across the team.

How the Skill Works

  1. 1InputEnter company name and website
  2. 2ResearchAnalyze public information
  3. 3MapMap products to the WEDA layers
  4. 4AlignAssess synergy with Advantech
  5. 5OutputGenerate an evaluation deck

Three Core Modules

1. WEDA Layer Mapping Matrix
  • Maps partner products to the five WEDA layers, categorizing each as Complement, Overlap, or Unknown in a visual matrix.
  • Identifies target industry verticals using public data, with source years for traceability.
2. Sales Kit & Partner Tier Alignment
  • Evaluates synergy with Advantech’s product lines and customer verticals.
  • Recommends a suitable Partner Program tier (Authorized or Certified) based on company size and business readiness, including ACC Marketplace listing requirements.
3. Visual & Action Plan Output
  • Converts evaluation results into presentation-ready slides, including partner positioning and PoC roadmaps.

Marketplace Optimisation

I worked on optimizing the Advantech Marketplace by aligning user needs, business objectives, and content presentation. This involved identifying gaps in the existing experience and planning UI and information architecture improvements to make solutions easier to discover, partner information more accessible, and platform content easier to maintain.

One example was the proposed Partner Use Case Catalog, a dedicated page designed to improve solution discovery and connect buyers directly with relevant partners.

Example Flow: Partner Use Case Catalog

Identify the Need for a New Page

Partner use cases were scattered across presentations, PDFs, and sales materials, making them difficult to discover and reuse. A dedicated catalog could address the needs of three key user groups:

  • Buyers: Find proven solutions by industry and connect directly with the partners behind them.
  • Partners: Gain visibility on the website, reinforcing a key benefit of the Partner Program.
  • Advantech Sales: Access a centralized reference to share relevant use cases without searching through scattered materials.

Design decision: Integrate the catalog into the partner web pages, linking each use case to its corresponding partner profile.

Audit Existing Content

Reviewed existing use cases from sales, marketing, and partners to assess content availability and readiness for publication.

  • Content Inventory: Consolidated existing cases and removed duplicates.
  • Publishing Requirements: Checked customer approval and confidentiality requirements, including anonymization where needed.
  • Content Taxonomy: Defined consistent tags for industry, capability, partner type, and region.
  • Content Gaps: Identified missing information, such as measurable project outcomes.

Output: A structured content inventory with standardized tags and publication status.

Define the User Experience

Translated content requirements into a page structure centered on three key elements:

  • Use Case Discovery: Filters by industry, capability, partner type, and region.
  • Consistent Content Structure: A unified Challenge → Solution → Value format for easier browsing and comparison.
  • Partner Connection: Direct links from each use case to the corresponding partner profile and contact options.

Output: Use case detail page wireframe, aligned with the UI team.

Design the Content Management Workflow

Planned a collaborative publishing workflow to ensure consistent content quality and long-term catalog maintenance.

  • Partner Submission: Partners submit project details, images, and customer approval through a structured form aligned with the catalog fields.
  • Advantech Review: Review content accuracy, tags, branding, and confidentiality, with feedback consolidated into one revision round.
  • Publication: Approved use cases are published in the catalog and linked to the corresponding partner profiles.

Master’s Thesis: AI Chatbots & Consumer Psychology

As AI chatbots become increasingly common in subscription retention, I explore whether conversational interfaces trigger stronger psychological reactance than static pages.

This study examines how retention interface design (AI chatbot vs. static page) and message framing (incentive-based vs. value-based) shape psychological reactance, service attitudes, and continued usage intentions.

Research Flow

Research Questions
  • Does a chatbot retention flow cause more psychological reactance than a static page?
  • Does the type of offer — a discount, or a reminder of unused features — change that effect?
  • Does reactance explain lower attitude toward the service and lower intent to keep using it?

Theory: Computers Are Social Actors Paradigm, Reactance Theory, Persuasion Knowledge Model.

Mobile Ear: Getting Around the City without Hearing the Announcements

People with hearing loss can miss important audio cues in urban environments, from upcoming transit stops to approaching emergency sirens. Mobile Ear transforms these sounds into visual and tactile alerts, helping users stay aware of their surroundings.

Design Process

User Interviews
  • Ten interviews with people with hearing loss about buses, the metro and emergencies.
  • Captured every observation on a sticky note for the next step.
Persona and Affinity Mapping
  • Clustered pain points into needs: knowing when to get off, and noticing danger they can’t hear.
  • Built a persona around those needs.
Ideate with 10+10
  • Sketched ten ideas, picked the strongest, then sketched ten variations of it.
  • Landed on two features: transit arrival alerts and emergency sound recognition, which I designed.
Annotated Wireframes
  • Laid out 16 screens across three flows — home, transit alerts and sound recognition — with numbered notes on what the user sees and feels at each step.

How Different Social Platforms Shape Different Kinds of Anxiety

A four-person research study exploring the relationship between social media use and three types of anxiety — appearance, social, and academic — among young people aged 16–24. The study examines whether these relationships differ across Instagram, Dcard, and Xiaohongshu.

400+survey responses on a 7-point Likert scale
15hypotheses tested
3 × 3platforms and anxiety types compared

Research Methods

1. Quantitative Survey Analysis
  • Collected 400+ survey responses using a 7-point Likert scale.
  • Measured appearance, social, and academic anxiety.
  • Examined correlations between platform usage time and average anxiety scores.
  • Tested 15 hypotheses using one-tailed statistical tests.
2. In-Depth Interviews
  • Interviewed participants who actively used all three platforms.
  • Applied grounded theory to explore how platform-specific experiences relate to different types of anxiety.
  • Used qualitative insights to provide context for the quantitative findings.

Finding

The relationships between social media usage time and anxiety varied across platforms and anxiety types. No single pattern applied equally.

The findings highlight the importance of considering both platform characteristics and different dimensions of anxiety when developing strategies to encourage healthier social media use.

What Excites Me about Starting My Career

What excites me about starting my career is the opportunity to see how technology can solve real problems and make everyday work better. I enjoy seeing how an idea can grow into something people actually use, and how technology can create value beyond what it promises on paper.

A Project I’m Proud Of

The AI partner assessment project is one I’m particularly proud of. Previously, every potential partner had to be researched and evaluated from scratch, so I developed a standardized assessment framework and explored how AI could help streamline the process.

One thing I learned was that automation only works when the thinking behind the process is clear. Before introducing AI, I first had to define what makes a good partner fit and what criteria we should use. In the end, the most important part wasn’t the AI itself, but building a clear framework that AI could actually work with.

My Strengths

One of my strengths is being able to turn unclear requirements into something concrete and actionable.

During my internship, I often worked with different stakeholders across business development, engineering, and external partners. They didn’t always communicate their needs in the same way, so I learned to clarify what each side actually needed, identify missing information, and translate those discussions into specifications, workflows, or action items.

I’m also someone who takes initiative when facing something unfamiliar. When I moved into the Partner Strategy role, I had to deal with a much broader scope than before. Instead of waiting for someone to guide me through everything, I learned to break down the problem, identify what I needed to know, and actively look for resources and people who could help.

I think these two qualities work well together. I can help move things forward while continuously learning what I need along the way.

Areas for Improvement

One thing I’m working on is finding a better balance between researching a topic thoroughly and moving things forward quickly.

When I receive an unfamiliar task, my first instinct is often to do a lot of research before putting together a first version. I want to understand the context and make sure I’m getting things right, but I’ve realized that spending too much time researching doesn’t always lead to a better outcome.

I’ve been trying to change this by setting a clearer boundary for my initial research and putting together an early draft or proposed structure sooner. Then, I can get feedback from stakeholders and figure out what actually needs further investigation.

I’m still working on this, but I’ve become more comfortable with the idea that a first version doesn’t have to be perfect. Sometimes, having something concrete to discuss is a better way to learn and move forward.
